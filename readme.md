# [Antenna Technology] Compressed Sensing Directional Array Matching Technique for Planar Sparse Arrays [with MATLAB Code]

Introduction: The comprehensive synthesis of large planar arrays is a classic problem in electromagnetics, attracting increasing attention over the years due to the high resolution required in both elevation and azimuth angles [1]-[12]. Despite this, the development of numerically efficient and robust methods remains an open research area [8]-[14], especially when dealing with complex problems, particularly when working with large irregular arrangements [8]-[17]. In practice, the joint optimization of element excitations and positions is used to meet user-defined requirements in terms of radiation pattern, overall size, and other aspects, leading to a strongly nonlinear problem whose dimensionality rapidly increases with the area of the planar aperture [6]-[18].
To effectively solve such challenging problems, several effective methods have been proposed in the past (see [4]-[21] and their references), although some difficulties have been encountered due to computational load when dealing with arbitrary geometric shapes of large arrangements (e.g., global optimization methods [3]-[8] or constraints on shape/element displacements [19]-[21]).
Regarding the synthesis of maximum sparse arrays, recently, a set of computationally efficient techniques has been introduced to design linear arrays [22]-[23]. In [22]-[23], array design has been appropriately formulated as a mode matching problem where the maximum sparse layout approximation is sought through a set of Bayesian Compressive Sensing (BCS) techniques [24]-[25]. Single-task (ST) [24]-[22] and multi-task (MT) [25]-[23] implementations consider both real and complex excitations. Due to its characteristics, the BCS techniques provide significant design flexibility, achieving near or improved performance compared to state-of-the-art linear sparse synthesis [22]-[23].
